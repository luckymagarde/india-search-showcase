What is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and how does it help businesses?+

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of optimizing content so it is more likely to be understood, synthesized, and referenced by generative AI systems — including AI-powered search results and chat-based assistants that generate answers rather than simply listing links. Where traditional SEO focuses primarily on ranking pages in a results list, GEO focuses on how well your content's facts, entities, and context can be extracted and used by AI systems constructing a response.

This involves several practical elements:

  • Clear entity definitions — describing your business, products and services consistently and unambiguously
  • Semantic relevance — using natural language that maps to how people actually ask questions
  • Structured content with clean headings and organization, plus supporting schema markup
  • Consistent information across your website and other online sources

For businesses, GEO is becoming an increasingly important complement to traditional SEO, since a growing share of research now happens through AI-generated summaries rather than a traditional list of results.

What is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and why is it important for modern search?+

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring content so it can be directly surfaced as an answer — in featured snippets, voice assistant responses, AI Overviews, and other answer-first search formats — rather than requiring a user to click through and search a page themselves. As more searches are resolved through a direct answer rather than a list of links, being the source of that answer matters more than it used to.

AEO typically involves writing content in a question-and-answer format where it makes sense, using clear and factual language that directly addresses common queries, implementing FAQ and HowTo schema markup, and structuring pages with logical headings that make the most relevant section easy to identify.

It's important because user behavior has shifted: people increasingly ask conversational questions and expect a direct answer. Businesses that structure content around genuine, clearly-answered questions are better positioned to be included in these formats, though no result can ever be guaranteed.

How can GEO and AEO help my business appear in AI-powered search results?+

GEO and AEO work together to improve the chances that AI-powered search tools — including AI Overviews, chat-based assistants, and voice search — reference or cite your business when generating a response. This isn't something that can be guaranteed, since AI systems draw on many sources and signals outside any single website's control, but there are concrete steps that meaningfully improve the odds.

These include maintaining consistent entity information about your business across your website and other online profiles, using structured data so machines can more easily parse key facts, writing content that directly and clearly answers real questions your customers ask, and building genuine topical authority rather than spreading content thin across unrelated topics.

E-E-A-T concepts — experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness — matter here too, since AI systems tend to favor sources that demonstrate real credibility. We combine this with the technical SEO foundation every website needs: fast page speed, clean semantic HTML, and accessible content.

Alongside this, we don't neglect the technical foundation: fast-loading pages, clean semantic HTML, and mobile-friendly design all make it easier for both crawlers and AI systems to access your content.

What is the difference between traditional SEO, AEO and GEO?+

Traditional SEO, AEO, and GEO are related but distinct disciplines that increasingly work together. Traditional SEO focuses on improving a website's visibility in classic search engine results pages — ranking for relevant keywords through technical optimization, content quality, and backlink authority, with the goal of earning clicks to your website.

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) focuses specifically on being featured as a direct answer — in featured snippets, voice assistant responses, or other answer-first formats — rather than simply ranking in a list. It relies heavily on question-based content structure and schema markup.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the newest of the three, focused on how AI systems that generate original responses understand, synthesize, and reference your content and brand. It places more weight on entity clarity, semantic relevance, and topical authority across your overall online presence.

In practice, these three approaches share a common foundation — technical health, clear content, and credible authority — which is why we build them together rather than as separate, competing strategies.

Can GEO and AEO work together with traditional Google SEO?+

Yes — in fact, GEO, AEO, and traditional SEO share enough of a common foundation that it rarely makes sense to treat them as separate strategies. All three depend on the same underlying fundamentals: a technically healthy website that's easy to crawl and fast to load, clearly written content that genuinely answers what people are searching for, and credible authority signals built through consistent, accurate information and relevant links.

Where they differ is mainly in emphasis. Traditional SEO puts more weight on keyword targeting and ranking positions. AEO adds a layer of question-based structure and schema markup aimed at answer-first search formats. GEO extends this further into entity clarity and semantic consistency for AI systems that generate original responses rather than listing sources.

Because of this overlap, most of the work we do — technical audits, content restructuring, schema implementation, and authority building — supports all three simultaneously, as one integrated strategy rather than separate, disconnected campaigns.

How does website speed and technical performance affect SEO and AI search visibility?+

Website speed and technical performance affect nearly every part of how a site is perceived by search engines, AI systems, and real visitors. Slow-loading pages tend to have higher bounce rates, which can indirectly hurt rankings, and Google has explicitly used Core Web Vitals — metrics covering loading speed, interactivity, and visual stability — as a ranking factor for years.

Technical performance also affects crawl efficiency: search engines allocate a limited crawl budget to each site, and slow or poorly optimized pages can mean fewer pages get crawled and indexed as quickly as they could be. For AI systems that need to process and extract information from a page, faster and cleaner code makes that process more reliable.

Our technical work in this area includes image and font optimization, reducing unnecessary JavaScript, implementing caching and lazy loading where appropriate, and regularly monitoring Core Web Vitals scores, since new content and features can quietly reintroduce speed issues over time.
